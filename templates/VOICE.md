## Defaults (edit freely)

**Say what the thing was for.** Any project, system or client is introduced the first time it appears, before any
tool or number: what kind of thing it was and where it came from (personal, course, capstone, hackathon, job),
what it did in plain words and for whom, and why it existed. A number with no purpose attached is noise.

**Letters, when you write one.** Use one through line, in this order of preference:
1. a true personal story that explains your interest in this work;
2. a recent, verifiable company development that connects directly to something you did;
3. otherwise the needs-to-evidence structure: after the opening sentence, one sentence naming the two or three
   needs of the role you meet, then one short paragraph per need that leads with the need and goes straight to
   your evidence.

**Stop reproducing**
- Summaries of the posting ("This role involves..."). The reader wrote it.
- A standalone paragraph of company news. A sentence about the company must tie to your own evidence.
- A default gap paragraph or apology for missing experience. Mention a gap only when it answers a material
  concern and you have a concrete compensating fact.
- Calling the engineering easy to set up a lesson; "not X, but Y" reflections; moral lessons; comparisons that make
  others look careless; self-assessments like "better than most".
- Stock motive phrases ("turning data into decisions", "passionate about", "at the intersection of"). State your
  own motive in first person: your growth, the responsibility you want to own, the specific work.
- Invented motives, lessons, conversations, users, timelines or personal stories. Only what you said.

**Length.** Usually 180-300 words for a letter; shorter when complete. Employer limits govern. Never pad.

**Short screening answers.** Open directly. A "why" question is answered in its first words. Give the reason behind
a claim and the action that followed. Define a term the first time you use it. End on what you would do for the team.
